The clinical barrier

Why single-target therapy falls short in solid tumors

Monoclonal antibodies directed at single oncogenic drivers frequently fail due to antigen loss, bypass signaling, and on-target off-tumor toxicities. Durable clinical responses require simultaneous dual-target engagement.

01

Antigen loss & escape mutations

Selective pressure from single-target therapeutics triggers receptor downregulation, alternative splicing, and target-negative clone emergence in solid tumors.

02

On-target, off-tumor toxicity

Individual target antigens are shared with healthy epithelial tissues, creating severe dose-limiting toxicities before therapeutic tumor saturation is achieved.

03

Compensatory pathway re-wiring

Inhibition of a single signaling node stimulates compensatory cross-talk through parallel growth receptor pathways, rapidly restoring tumor survival.

"Single-antigen blockades exert evolutionary bottlenecks that heterogeneous tumors circumvent. Next-generation oncology demands obligate dual-target coincidence detection."
